## What sitting does to your lower back

If your back feels fine in the morning and wrecked by 3 PM, your chair is a big part of the story. Sitting puts more pressure on the discs in your lower back than standing does, and most of us sit eight hours or more a day.

When you sit, your pelvis tips back and the natural curve in your lower back flattens. That shifts load onto the front of your discs and stretches the ligaments behind them. One hour of that is no big deal. A full workday, five days a week, adds up.

Over time the muscles that hold you upright get lazy, and the hip flexors at the front of your hips get short and tight. Tight hip flexors pull on your lower spine every time you stand, which is why the first few steps after a long meeting often hurt the most.


## Signs it is more than a stiff back

- Pain that travels into your buttock or down one leg
- Numbness or tingling in your foot
- Pain that gets worse when you cough or sneeze
- Stiffness that lasts more than 30 minutes after you get up

> “Most of the low back pain I see from desk work is a load problem, not a damage problem. Change the load and the pain usually follows.”
>
> Casey Morgan, D.C.

## Simple changes that help

You do not need a standing desk or a new chair to make progress. Start with these four habits:

1. Stand up every 30 minutes, even for 60 seconds
2. Keep your hips slightly higher than your knees
3. Raise your screen to eye level so you stop leaning forward
4. Walk for 10 minutes after lunch
